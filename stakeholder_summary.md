# Stakeholder Summary: Sales and Profit Analysis

**Headline:** Between September 2013 and December 2014 the business earned **16.89M profit on 118.73M of sales (14.2% margin)**. Two-thirds of that profit comes from one segment, and one segment loses money.

## What the data shows

1. **Government is the engine.** It is 44% of sales and 67% of profit (11.39M profit, 21.7% margin).
2. **Enterprise loses money** (-0.61M overall). Its list price is 125 per unit and its cost is 120, so any discount above about 4% makes an order loss-making. The average Enterprise discount is 6.9%. All 58 loss-making orders in the data are Enterprise orders at Medium or High discounts.
3. **Deep discounts cut margin across the whole business.** Margin falls from 21.9% with no discount to 9.1% in the High band. High-discount orders are 31.5% of sales but only 20.1% of profit.
4. **The United States sells the most but earns the least per dollar:** 25.03M of sales at a 12.0% margin, with the largest Enterprise loss (-175K).
5. **Growth is healthy.** Comparing the same months (Sep-Dec), sales grew 36.9% and profit grew 40.1% from 2013 to 2014. (2013 has only four months of data, so full-year totals should not be compared.)

## What we recommend

- Cap Enterprise discounts at the Low band, or raise the Enterprise list price toward 129. Up to +0.78M profit.
- Require approval for High-band discounts in every segment.
- Review Small Business costs (35.7% of sales, only 9.8% margin) and US pricing.
- Protect Government, and test growth in Channel Partners and Midmarket, which have high margins on a small base.

## Decision requested

Approve a discount-policy review for Enterprise and High-band orders.
